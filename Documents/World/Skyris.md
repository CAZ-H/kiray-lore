Skyris is a K1V type star that is ~79% of the mass of Sol, and ~79% of its radius. The habitable zone of Skyris is within 0.60 - 0.86 AU. It is ~5.8 billion years old. 

The temperature of Skyris is ~5155 K. Because it is cooler and smaller than Sol, Skyris burns slowly and steadily, giving it a lifespan far longer than a Sol-type star and a calmer output of harmful radiation. Its light is slightly redder than Sol, appearing warm white to pale orange. The luminosity of Skyris is ~39% of Sol.

Skyris has a lifespan of ~20 billion years, almost double of Sol's lifespan, and only ~29% through it. About ~1.7 million tonnes of mass is converted into energy per second, compared to ~4.3 million tonnes for Sol.

From Kiray, Skyris appears about 25% wider than Sol does from Earth.

Skyris rotates once about every 38 days at its equator.

## The Solar System

The Skyris System consists of 5 major bodies. Only the [[Kiray|moon of the second body]] in the system is capable of sustaining life.

In order from the sun, the Skiedonic people have recorded the bodies in the system under the names: 
1. **Skel:** a hot, volcanic, sulfurous planet.
2. **[[Kete]]:** a striking purple gas giant.
	- Kete hosts [[Kiray]], the subject of this archive.
3. **Siirk:** an arid barren planet, rich in iron, nickel, and silicates.
	- Siirk hosts Si, a small barren moon. 
4. **Vol:** a massive, deep blue gas giant, almost twice the diameter of Kete.
	- Vol hosts the smaller Avo'ton and the larger Avo'rii, two barren moons. The names of these moons simply reflect their numbered positions in [[Skiedonic_Numeral_System|Skiedonic]].
5. **Avo:** a deep striated red gas giant with particulate rings, slightly smaller than Kete. The name also seems to reflect naming patterns from the numeral system.

Finally, a thin, stable belt of asteroids and space debris encircles the Skyris system.

**References (Meta)**

- Sol values are IAU nominal ([Prša et al. 2016](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/0004-6256/152/2/41/pdf)). Sol's mass is calculated from the nominal `GM` divided by the CODATA value of `G`.
- Skyris is modelled on [GJ 86](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanet-catalog/gj-86-b/) (also called HD 13445), a real K1V or K1.5V star. Skyris's radius, mass, temperature, and luminosity are within the published measurement range for GJ 86.
- Skyris's spectral class reflects [Mamajek's dwarf sequence](https://www.pas.rochester.edu/~emamajek/EEM_dwarf_UBVIJHK_colors_Teff.txt).
- Skyris's temperature and age are chosen.
- Skyris's mass and luminosity are calculated so that [[Kiray]] receives nearly the same light as Earth with a 204.20-day year. They use the mass-luminosity relation, `L = M^4` in solar units, and Kepler's third law.
- Skyris's radius is calculated from the Stefan-Boltzmann law, `L = 4 * pi * R^2 * sigma * T^4`.
- Skyris's peak wavelength is calculated from Wien's law, `wavelength = b/T`.
- Skyris's habitable zone is Sol's 0.95 - 1.37 AU, scaled by `sqrt(L)`.
- Details derived with help from Alliant Tallisar / @Tallisar on Roblox:
	- Skyris's surface gravity is calculated from `g = G * M / R^2`.
	- Skyris's density is its mass divided by its volume.
	- Skyris's main sequence lifespan is estimated from `10 * M^-2.5` in solar units. The value is approximate.
	- Skyris's mass conversion rate is calculated from `L / c^2`.
	- Skyris's size seen from Kiray is calculated from its radius and Kete's distance away.
	- Skyris's rotation period is chosen to match a typical star of its age and color, using the gyrochronology relation of [Mamajek & Hillenbrand (2008)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.1686), which shows that similar real stars have a rotation period of 35-45 days.
	- Skyris's rotation velocity and rotation rate are calculated from its radius and rotation period.
	- Skyris's surface area and volume are calculated from its radius.
	- Skyris's energy production per kg is `L/M`, and its surface emission is `sigma * T^4`.
	- Skyris's absolute bolometric magnitude is calculated from `4.74 - 2.5 * log10(L)` in solar units.
	- Skyris's metallicity is taken from the model star, GJ 86.
