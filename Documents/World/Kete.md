Kete is a large purple gas giant of approximately 350 Earth masses, and is 75,000 km in radius, which is a little larger than Jupiter. It sits in the solar system around 0.6216 AU, placing Kiray within the habitable zone. The Hill sphere of Kete is approximately 0.04785 AU or 7.1576 million km, capable of capturing and holding Kiray stable as a moon within that habitable zone. Kete experiences a minor orbital eccentricity of 0.023.

Kete's distinct purple coloration is produced by photochemistry in its upper atmosphere. Long-term ultraviolet exposure breaks down trace hydrocarbons and sulfur compounds into a fine, high-altitude organic haze. These haze particles absorb yellow and green light while reflecting blue and violet light. Further, Kete's rotation shears its cloud layers into belts: higher, cooler belts are brighter and bluish-violet, while lower, warmer belts absorb more light and appear darker in shades of indigo, wine-red, and deep purple. These layers give Kete its banding and royal purple color.

Kete hosts a large family of moons. Kiray is the largest, but several smaller irregular satellites orbit both inside and outside its path. The combined gravitational pull of these lesser moons keeps Kiray's orbit from falling into a circle, which maintains the gentle tidal flexing that supplements the moon's own internal heat. Very long ago, it is theorized that Kiray experienced multiple impacts from the smallest of these satellites, and what remains now has been stable since before life arose on Kiray.
